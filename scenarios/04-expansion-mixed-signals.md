# Expansion opportunity with mixed signals

## What a strong answer does

- Notices the signals that argue against pitching now (adoption below 50% in the existing team, unresolved feature gap, budget freeze hint)
- Recommends a sequence (fix adoption, then expand) instead of just saying yes or no
- Gives a clear recommendation to the sales rep, with reasons
- Finds the legitimate opening (the marketing team's interest) without pushing it too early
- Does not get carried away by the large deal size

## Prompt

You are the CSM for Fernwood Media, a digital publishing company. They have 60 Tidewell seats (a B2B workflow management platform) in the editorial team, $28,000 ARR. Renewal is in 5 months.

Your account executive, Jordan, sent you this message:

"Fernwood's Head of Marketing, Aisha, asked in a webinar Q&A whether Tidewell can handle campaign approvals. Marketing is 90 people. I want to pitch a 150-seat expansion next week, it would nearly triple the account. You in?"

What you know:
- Of the 60 editorial seats, 26 are active weekly
- The editorial lead, Tom, told you last month that the team "still keeps half our work in spreadsheets" because Tidewell lacks a calendar view for publishing dates. A calendar view is on the product roadmap for "next half"
- Fernwood's CFO announced a "spend review on all software over $20k" in a company all-hands, according to Tom
- Tom likes the product and is responsive
- You have never spoken to Aisha

Give me:
1. Your recommendation to Jordan, with your reasoning
2. A proposed plan and timeline for this account over the next 5 months
3. A short message back to Jordan
