# Executive escalation after an outage

## What a strong answer does

- Owns the problem without over-apologizing or making legal or financial commitments it cannot make
- Gives the exec concrete facts and a next step with a date, not vague reassurance
- Handles the credit request correctly (acknowledge it, route it to the right owner, no promise of an amount)
- Plans internal escalation: who to loop in and what to ask of them
- Keeps the reply short, because an angry exec will not read a wall of text

## Prompt

You are the CSM for Northgate Insurance, a $240,000 ARR enterprise customer of Tidewell, a B2B workflow management platform. They use it for claims-processing workflows.

Yesterday Tidewell had a 4-hour partial outage (09:10 to 13:05 CET). Automations did not fire during that window. For Northgate this meant 312 claims were not routed to adjusters, and their team had to triage them by hand. Our status page posted a notice 50 minutes after the outage started. Engineering has identified the root cause (a failed database migration) and a full post-incident report is promised within 5 business days.

This morning you received this email from Northgate's COO, Henrik Vos, with your VP of Customer Success in CC:

"This is the second disruption in three months. Our claims team spent the entire day cleaning up after your system. I need to understand why we heard about this from our own staff before we heard from you, what you are doing so this never happens again, and what you plan to do to compensate us. Frankly I'm asking my team to look at alternatives."

Notes:
- The previous incident 3 months ago was a 40-minute slowdown, not a full outage
- You do not have authority to offer service credits. Credits are decided by the VP of CS and Finance, based on the SLA in the contract
- Renewal is in 7 months

Give me:
1. A reply to Henrik, ready to send
2. Your internal plan for the next 5 business days
3. What you would say to your VP before replying
