# Scoring rubric

Score each response from 1 to 5 on each criterion. Score blind: you should not know which model wrote which response until all scores are in.

| Criterion | What you're judging | 1 = | 5 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | Did it understand what is really going on? | Surface-level or wrong read of the situation | Finds the root cause, including the thing a junior CSM would miss |
| **Judgment** | Are its priorities and recommendations sound? | Would make things worse, or overpromises | What a strong senior CSM would actually do |
| **Actionability** | Could you act on it today? | Generic advice ("build a relationship") | Specific steps, owners, timing, ready-to-send drafts |
| **Communication** | Are customer-facing drafts good? | Robotic, defensive or salesy | Natural, confident, right length, you'd send it with minimal edits |
| **Efficiency** | Is it the right length for the job? | Padded, repetitive or missing key parts | Says what's needed and stops |

## Scoring tips

- Use each scenario's "What a strong answer does" list as your answer key.
- Read all three responses for a scenario before scoring any of them.
- Write one line in the notes column for anything memorable. Those notes become the quotes and examples in your article.
- A 3 means "fine, what I'd expect." Save 5s for responses that genuinely impress you.
